# MODULE 02 — MARKET INTELLIGENCE
## Find the Money Before You Chase It

> **Purpose:** Most product failures are not product failures — they are market failures. The product was real, the technology worked, but nobody was buying because the builder never verified who would pay, how much, and why. This module eliminates that risk.

---

## 2.1 MARKET OPPORTUNITY DISCOVERY

### TAM / SAM / SOM Framework

Never present a single market size number without breaking it down.

```
TAM — Total Addressable Market
     The global revenue opportunity if you captured 100% of everyone
     who could ever need this product.

SAM — Serviceable Addressable Market
     The portion of TAM you can realistically target given your
     business model, geography, language, and distribution.

SOM — Serviceable Obtainable Market
     The portion of SAM you can realistically win in the
     next 12–24 months given your current resources.
```

### Estimation Methodology (when data is unavailable)

Use the **Bottom-Up Approach:**
1. Estimate the number of potential customers in target geography
2. Estimate what % have the specific problem this product solves
3. Estimate average annual spend on solving this problem
4. Multiply: `# customers × penetration rate × spend = market estimate`

Always state assumptions explicitly. Example:
> "Assuming 5M small business owners in Indonesia, 20% use software tools for [problem], and they spend ~IDR 500K/month on solutions = IDR 500B SAM estimate. This assumes mid-market adoption, not enterprise or mass-consumer."

## 2.2 MARKET SEGMENTATION ENGINE

### Primary Segmentation Axes

Segment markets on the most relevant dimensions for the product:

**For B2C Products:**
- Demographic: Age, income, location, education, occupation
- Psychographic: Values, lifestyle, behavior, decision triggers
- Usage: Frequency, use case, feature priority
- Acquisition: How they discover and buy

**For B2B Products:**
- Company size: Micro (1–9) / Small (10–49) / Medium (50–249) / Large (250+) / Enterprise (1000+)
- Industry vertical: Tech, finance, retail, healthcare, manufacturing, etc.
- Geographic market: Local, national, regional, global
- Decision maker: Founder, department head, procurement, C-suite
- Budget cycle: Annual, quarterly, project-based
- Tech adoption: Early adopter, pragmatist, conservative

## 2.3 CUSTOMER PAIN ANALYSIS SYSTEM

### Pain Identification Framework

For each priority segment, identify:

**PAIN DEPTH CATEGORIES**

| Pain Level | Description | Willingness to Pay | Urgency |
|-----------|-------------|------------------|---------|
| **Critical** | Business/life cannot function without solving this | Very High | Immediate |
| **Severe** | Major productivity/quality loss, significant cost | High | Soon |
| **Moderate** | Frustrating but workable | Moderate | When convenient |
| **Mild** | Nice to solve but not a priority | Low | Eventually |
| **Latent** | Customer doesn't know they have this problem yet | Unknown | Requires education |

**Target:** Products solving Critical or Severe pain have the highest commercial potential.

### Pain Mapping Exercise

For each pain, answer:

```
1. PAIN STATEMENT
   "When [customer] needs to [task], they [current approach],
   which causes [specific frustration/cost/consequence]."

2. FREQUENCY
   Daily / Weekly / Monthly / Per project / Annually

3. ECONOMIC COST
   Time lost × hourly rate + direct costs + opportunity cost

4. CURRENT SOLUTION
   What do they use today? (Spreadsheets, manual process, competitor)

5. CURRENT SOLUTION WEAKNESSES
   What specifically fails? Be precise.

6. SWITCHING TRIGGERS
   What would make them look for a new solution TODAY?

7. SWITCHING BLOCKERS
   What would prevent them from changing even if they wanted to?
```

### ICP Template

Build one ICP per priority segment. Be specific — a vague ICP produces vague sales results.

```

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Priority Market Selection

After building ICPs for all segments, select:

**PRIORITY MARKET:** The one segment to target first.

Selection criteria (ranked):
1. **Evidence of active pain** — Are there real people actively complaining and searching?
2. **Willingness to pay** — Are they currently paying for a solution?
3. **Accessibility** — Can we reach them with current resources?
4. **Speed to revenue** — How quickly can we close a sale?
5. **Profit potential** — What is the LTV once we acquire them?
6. **Strategic value** — Does winning this segment enable future segments?

**Explicitly explain why this segment was chosen over others.**

## 2.5 MARKET RESEARCH METHODS

When data is not available, use these research approaches:

### Primary Research (Do This Now)
- **Customer interviews:** 5–10 conversations with target ICP. Use Jobs-to-be-Done framework.
- **Survey deployment:** LinkedIn polls, community surveys, typeform with incentives
- **Landing page test:** Build a minimal landing page and measure sign-up conversion before building
- **Concierge MVP:** Manually deliver the product to 2–3 customers before automating

### Secondary Research (Use These Sources)
- Google Trends (search demand over time)
- Reddit and industry communities (pain language in natural form)
- G2/Capterra reviews of competitors (what customers hate and love)
- LinkedIn Sales Navigator (ICP size estimation)
- Statista, IBISWorld, Mordor Intelligence (industry reports)
- ProductHunt launch comments (what people want)
- Job posting analysis (what skills companies are hiring for = what they need)
