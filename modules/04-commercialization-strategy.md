# MODULE 04 — COMMERCIALIZATION STRATEGY
## Turning a Product Into a Revenue-Generating Business

> **Purpose:** Most products fail not because they are bad, but because their commercialization model is wrong. The right product sold through the wrong model — wrong pricing, wrong channel, wrong buyer — will fail every time. This module determines the optimal path from product to revenue.

## 4.2 BUSINESS MODEL EVALUATION MATRIX

### Model Definitions

**A. DIRECT SALES — TRANSACTIONAL**
One-time sale directly to end customer.
- Best for: Physical products, digital downloads, software licenses
- Economics: High margin per sale, no recurring revenue
- Challenge: Must continuously acquire new customers

**B. SUBSCRIPTION (SaaS / Access)**
Recurring fee for ongoing access or service.
- Best for: Software, content platforms, services with ongoing value
- Economics: Predictable MRR, high LTV, requires churn management
- Challenge: Must prove value continuously to prevent cancellation

**C. MARKETPLACE DISTRIBUTION**
Selling through third-party platforms.
- Best for: Consumer products, digital assets, apps, games
- Economics: Lower margin (platform takes 15–30%), but instant access to large audience
- Challenge: Platform dependency, pricing pressure, limited customer relationships

**D. B2B DIRECT SALES**
Sales-led motion targeting business buyers.
- Best for: Enterprise software, professional tools, business services
- Economics: High ACV (annual contract value), longer sales cycles, high CAC
- Challenge: Requires sales infrastructure, long time-to-revenue

**E. B2G (GOVERNMENT)**
Procurement, tender, and contract-based government sales.
- Best for: Compliance tools, infrastructure, educational tech, security
- Economics: Very large contracts, slow to close, high compliance burden
- Challenge: 6–24 month sales cycles, heavy compliance requirements

**F. LICENSING**
Sell rights to use the product, technology, or IP.
- Best for: Proprietary technology, content, patents, brands
- Economics: High margin, recurring if structured correctly, low operational overhead
- Challenge: Requires strong IP protection, complex agreement negotiation

**G. WHITE-LABEL**
Allow other companies to sell your product under their brand.
- Best for: Software infrastructure, B2B tools, any product with broad applicability
- Economics: Wholesale pricing, volume deals, recurring fees
- Challenge: Product must be highly configurable; requires partner management

**H. RESELLER / DISTRIBUTOR NETWORK**
Build an indirect sales channel through partners.
- Best for: Physical products, regional market penetration, complex technical products
- Economics: Lower per-unit margin, but massive scale potential
- Challenge: Partner recruitment, training, margin sharing, channel conflicts

**I. FREEMIUM**
Free base tier + paid premium features.
- Best for: Software, apps, tools with broad consumer/SMB appeal
- Economics: High user acquisition, conversion typically 2–5% free-to-paid
- Challenge: Infrastructure costs for free users; must have clear upgrade trigger

**J. AFFILIATE / REFERRAL**
Third parties earn commission for driving customers.
- Best for: B2C digital products, marketplaces, consumer apps
- Economics: Pay for performance; lower risk but requires attractive commissions
- Challenge: Affiliate quality management, attribution tracking

**K. PLATFORM / MARKETPLACE CREATION**
Build a two-sided marketplace around the product.
- Best for: Products that connect buyers and sellers
- Economics: Transaction fees, subscription, advertising — but requires both sides
- Challenge: Classic chicken-and-egg problem; very capital intensive

**L. SERVICES WRAPPER**
Sell the product as part of a service engagement.
- Best for: Complex products requiring implementation, consulting, training
- Economics: Higher per-customer value; not scalable without team growth
- Challenge: Time-bound revenue; difficult to productize

## 4.4 MODEL COMBINATION STRATEGY

The most successful businesses often use multiple models in sequence or in parallel.

### Common Model Combinations

**FREEMIUM → SUBSCRIPTION**
Start free to build user base, convert to paid with premium features.
Adoption sequence: Free → Engaged users → Feature paywall → Subscription

**DIRECT SALES → CHANNEL SALES**
Build product-market fit through direct sales, then scale via resellers/distributors.
Adoption sequence: 10 direct customers → Prove value → Recruit partners → Scale

**B2C → B2B**
Start with individual users who bring the product into their workplace.
Classic examples: Slack, Figma, Zoom — entered through individuals, closed enterprise deals

**TRANSACTIONAL → SUBSCRIPTION**
Launch with one-time purchases to reduce friction, then introduce recurring option.
Example: Sell premium template once, then offer new templates subscription

**SERVICES → PRODUCT**
Begin with consulting to understand real problems, then productize the solution.
Benefit: Revenue while building, customers as development partners

## 4.6 UNIT ECONOMICS VIABILITY CHECK

Before committing to a model, verify the economics work:

### Direct Sales Check
```
Avg Sale Price (ASP): [X]
Cost of Goods Sold (COGS): [Y]
Gross Margin: [(X-Y)/X × 100]%
Sales & Marketing Cost per Customer: [Z]
Gross Profit per Customer: [X - Y - Z]

→ Is Gross Profit per Customer positive? If not, this model loses money at any scale.
```

### Subscription Check
```
Monthly Recurring Revenue per Customer (MRR): [X]
Monthly Churn Rate: [Y]%
Average Customer Lifetime (months): [1/Y]
LTV (Lifetime Value): [X × (1/Y)]
Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC): [Z]
LTV:CAC Ratio: [LTV/Z]

→ Target LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1 for a sustainable subscription business
→ Payback Period (CAC/MRR) should be < 12 months
```

### Marketplace Check
```
Gross Sale Price: [X]
Platform Commission: [Y]%
Your Revenue: [X × (1-Y)]
COGS: [Z]
Net per Transaction: [X(1-Y) - Z]

→ Are margins sufficient after platform fee? Many physical goods fail this test.
```
